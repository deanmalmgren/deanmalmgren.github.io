## The situation

In 2009 I co-founded Datascope to design algorithms that are valuable for businesses and useful for people. This was before “data science” was a common job title, and well before AI was something every company had a strategy for.

## What I did

- **Made the early bets.** Which customers to take on first, how to price the work, and how to hire. At the start it was “a really rich ocean of possibilities.”
- **Developed a practice.** Through client work, writing, and public speaking, we developed techniques for human-centered data science. That approach carried into IDEO’s AI × Design practice after the acquisition.
- **Built a culture.** Humility, collaboration, and transparency. We took pride in growing data scientists into project leads who could run client work.
- **Built in the open.** We created open source tools like [textract](https://github.com/deanmalmgren/textract) (about 4.7k GitHub stars) and [scrubadub](https://github.com/LeapBeyond/scrubadub), and organized the Chicago Data Science Meetup.
- **Learned new domains fast.** Client work took us into food and beverage, banking and finance, agriculture, pharmaceuticals, and healthcare.

## What happened

- We bootstrapped Datascope to 15 people and grew it 50% a year for its last four years.
- IDEO acquired Datascope in 2017 ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90147010/exclusive-ideos-plan-to-stage-an-ai-revolution)).
- “I remember getting off the call and pretty much breaking down into tears of joy and satisfaction and a job well done over a long period of time.”

## What I’d tell you

When you found a company, its culture reflects how you want to work, its customers reflect the impact you want to have, and its investors reflect who you want alongside you. I’d do it again.
