## The situation

Planet FWD needed product decisions that drove revenue and retention, with a small team and a market maturing more slowly than expected. LCAs for animal systems (livestock, with enteric fermentation, feed production, and manure management) had been a long-standing request from our science team. It would be an expensive feature to build.

## What I did

- **Made the calls, and explained them.** I took input from across the company, and I could clearly say why we would do some things and not others. In the end, it was my call.
- **Made delivery and value visible.** We built a company-wide product forecast, integrated with Jira, that simulated how long epics would take from their t-shirt sizes. In our “boulders” meeting we put a revenue figure on each epic, so we were honest about whether something was worth it “or if it was just a cool thing that might unlock some potential, maybe, segment that wasn’t real.”
- **Waited for the signal.** For a long time animal systems never had the ROI. Then 10–15 opportunities that needed it showed up within two months. The math changed, and we built it.
- **Brought customers in.** I started a customer council: every 3–6 months we interviewed a handful of valued customers about features we were weighing. When we had to choose between investing in LCAs or carbon accounting, the council made it clear that LCAs were much closer to full self-service, so we invested there, including agentic features that help customers finish on their own.
- **Segmented honestly.** I grouped customers by where they were on their climate journey: crawlers, walkers, runners, and flyers. We built for the flyers, betting the market would move that way quickly.

## What happened

- We won about half of those animal-systems opportunities and booked about $400k that year, opening a segment we might not otherwise have reached.
- Prioritized features consistently returned 8–10× their cost.
- The flyer bet was wrong on timing: the market has matured slowly. But because we integrated LCAs with carbon accounting, we kept that segment, with renewal revenue retention above 100% three years running.

## What I’d tell you

A roadmap with revenue attached makes “no” a lot easier to say, and makes “yes” obvious when the market finally moves.
