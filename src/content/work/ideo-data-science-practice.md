## The situation

When IDEO acquired Datascope in 2017, I had to keep the team together and grow data science across a design firm organized around studios. Each studio’s managing director owned the P&L, so I had no formal authority over people or budgets. AI was just starting to take off.

## What I did

- **Held a consistent vision.** Bringing a more human-centered approach to data science and AI. It motivated people, especially through the client work.
- **Led through influence.** I supported every project team doing AI work and partnered with the 5–15 directors who owned client relationships at any given time, supporting a community of 30-plus data scientists at its peak.
- **Developed people into leaders.** At Datascope we were proud of turning “nerdy data scientists” into project leads who could run client work. At IDEO we went further and developed several of them into directors and senior directors who owned the data science and AI portfolio at studios around the world.
- **Led several multi-million-dollar client relationships** across healthcare, beauty, CPG, food and beverage, financial services, insurance, furniture, and consumer electronics, in North America, South America, Europe, and Asia.

## What happened

- We kept 100% of the Datascope team and doubled the number of data scientists in three years, reaching nearly every IDEO market.
- AI × Design revenue doubled across the firm.
- People want to work with me again: I later hired a former IDEO direct report at Planet FWD, and others have asked to join.

## What I’d tell you

“You won’t hear me tell a story about how somebody threatened to leave and I kept them. I generally find that it’s most effective to keep people excited and motivated through an exciting vision and the work that we do.”
